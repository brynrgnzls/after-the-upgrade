# After the Upgrade — Website Overview

## What this website is about

**After the Upgrade** is a sustainable-technology advocacy website. It encourages people to treat electronics as valuable, maintainable resources instead of disposable products.

Its central message is:

> “A gadget’s usefulness should not end when you upgrade.”

The campaign promotes a practical three-step approach:

1. **Repair it** when a device can still serve its purpose.
2. **Reuse it** by donating, repurposing, or passing along working technology.
3. **Return it responsibly** through safe, verified electronic-waste recovery when a device has reached its end of life.

The website combines explainers, checklists, interactive decision support, and shareable campaign resources to help visitors make safer and less wasteful choices about phones, laptops, batteries, chargers, and other electronics.

## Why it exists

Modern upgrade cycles often lead people to replace devices that could be maintained, repaired, upgraded, donated, or reused. This contributes to electronic waste, loss of recoverable materials, unsafe battery disposal, and unnecessary demand for newly manufactured hardware.

After the Upgrade addresses this issue by making responsible technology stewardship easier to understand and act on. Rather than only describing the problem, the site helps visitors answer practical questions such as:

- Is this device worth repairing?
- How can I make my phone or laptop last longer?
- What should I do with a swollen battery?
- How do I erase my data before giving away or recycling a device?
- How can I tell whether a recycler is trustworthy?
- What can my school, family, office, or community do next?

## Who the website serves

The website is designed for a broad group of people who own, use, manage, or advocate for technology:

- **Students and youth advocates** who want to practice device care or organize campus initiatives.
- **Everyday gadget owners** deciding whether to repair, replace, sell, donate, or recycle a device.
- **Families** sorting through unused electronics, batteries, cables, and chargers.
- **Schools and computer laboratories** planning equipment triage, refurbishment, and collection activities.
- **Offices and small businesses** preparing devices for retirement and data-safe disposal.
- **Technology enthusiasts and makers** interested in modular design, repair culture, creative reuse, and right-to-repair advocacy.

## Key interactive features

### Device Decision Matrix

The decision tool helps visitors make a realistic choice about an older device. It considers the device’s condition and likely value of repair, then guides the visitor toward an appropriate action such as maintenance, repair, reuse, donation, or responsible recycling.

### Data-safety checklist

Before a device changes hands, visitors can work through an eight-step data-safety checklist. The guidance emphasizes backing up important files, removing accounts, handling activation locks, and using the correct reset or wipe approach for the device.

### Personal action dashboard

The Take Action page provides sixteen achievable challenges across several stewardship categories. Visitors can mark actions complete, monitor their progress, and record a campaign pledge.

### Campaign toolkit

The toolkit makes it easier to spread the message beyond the website. It offers adaptable social content and activity ideas for classrooms, peer groups, organizations, and community events.

## A typical visitor journey

1. A visitor arrives with an old or problematic device.
2. They use the **Device Decision Matrix** to decide whether it should be repaired, reused, donated, or retired.
3. If the device is usable, they visit **Extend Device Life** for care and maintenance guidance.
4. If it will leave their possession, they visit **Protect Your Data** to prepare it safely.
5. If it cannot be reused, they consult **Safe Electronic Disposal** for battery handling and recycler-vetting advice.
6. They can then make a pledge, complete action challenges, or use the toolkit to involve others.

## Core values

The site is built around several connected principles:

- **Longevity over disposability:** devices should be maintained and repaired when practical.
- **Reuse before recycling:** working hardware should remain useful through repurposing or donation when possible.
- **Safety and accountability:** batteries, personal data, and hazardous components require careful handling.
- **Informed decisions:** visitors should understand the trade-offs behind repair, replacement, donation, and disposal.
- **Community participation:** sustainable technology habits grow through schools, homes, workplaces, repair groups, and public advocacy.

## Privacy approach

After the Upgrade is designed as a local-first educational website. It does not require users to create an account or submit personal information. Progress, pledges, and device-decision records are stored only in the visitor’s browser through IndexedDB when available. Visitors can clear that local campaign data from the Take Action page.

The site does not rely on a remote database, third-party tracking, or analytics services for these interactive features.

## In one sentence

**After the Upgrade helps people extend the useful life of electronics, protect their data, and ensure that devices are reused or recycled responsibly instead of becoming avoidable e-waste.**
